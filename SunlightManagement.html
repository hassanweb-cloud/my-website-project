Sunlight Management

Track your garden's sun exposure to position full-sun plants vs. shade-loving variants correctly.

The Art of Mapping Exposure

Sunlight shifts continuously throughout the day and changes drastically with each passing season. Before putting seeds or roots in the ground, tracking your garden spaces' layout determines whether your plants will thrive or wither away.

By spending a few days monitoring how shadows fall from fences, walls, and trees, you can create a reliable "sun map" to divide your garden beds strategically.

Understanding Light Categories

6+ Hours Direct Light

Full Sun Zones

Best reserved for fruiting crops and vibrant blossoms like tomatoes, peppers, sunflowers, and Mediterranean herbs. These variants need uninhibited mid-day solar rays to feed photosynthesis and produce robust yields.

3 to 6 Hours Light

Partial Shade Options

Ideal for crisp, leafy greens (lettuce, spinach, kale) and root root vegetable crops. They enjoy morning sunrays but require structural relief from the burning, harsh heat of mid-afternoon windows.

Under 3 Hours Light

Full Shade Solutions

Tailored for under-canopy specimens like ferns, hostas, and mosses. These structures thrive in reflected, ambient illumination and can suffer severe leaf-scorch or bleached textures if left under bare sky rays.

Reading Leaf Signs

  • Too Little Light: Leggy, thin, stretching stems with abnormally pale leaves as the plant reaches out for illumination.
  • Too Much Light: Bleached white or crisp brown scorch spots in the center patches of leaves exposed to midday heat.

Orientation Guide

Depending on geographic layout:

  • South-Facing: Recieves maximum heat and continuous illumination.
  • North-Facing: Predominantly shady, cool, and slow drying.
  • East-Facing: Gentle, cool morning sun; good for delicate blooms.
